ALTERNATIVE NAMES:

LOCATION:

DISTRICT / REGION:

CATEGORY:
Historical Site / Archaeological Site

OVERVIEW:
The Barabar Caves in Jehanabad are a highly significant archaeological site featuring exquisite ancient rock-cut architecture. Carved out of solid rocks during the 3rd century BCE, they bear details of Lord Buddha's life and are considered exquisite examples of Mauryan artisanship.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
These caves have been chiselled out from massive solid rocks. They are widely celebrated for their highly polished interiors, which shine with an amazing brilliance characteristic of Mauryan polish. The caves bear artistic details related to the life of the Buddha. The complex includes several specific caves such as Sudama Cave, Lomash Rishi Cave, Vishwamitra Cave (Visva Zopari Cave), and Nagarjuna Cave (Gopika, Vapi, and Vedathika).

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Carved in the 3rd century BCE, the caves are exquisite examples of the craftsmanship of artisans from the Mauryan times. They are among the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India, reflecting profound archaeological importance.

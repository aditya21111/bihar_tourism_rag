OVERVIEW:
Kolhua is a famous Buddhist excavation site situated approximately 65 km North-West of Patna in Vaishali. The excavations have revealed the famous Ashokan Pillar having a life-size statue of a lion on the top, along with many other articles related to Buddhism. The site commemorates Buddha's last sermon and holds immense historical and religious significance.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Emperor Ashoka built "The Lion Pillar" at Kolhua. It is made of a highly polished single piece of red sandstone, surmounted by a bell-shaped capital, and stands 18.3 meters high. A life-size figure of a lion is placed on top of the pillar. The pillar is located beside a brick stupa at Kolhua. The area also contains a small tank known as Ramkund. 

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The site is highly significant for Buddhists as it commemorates Buddha's last sermon. The excavations have revealed many articles related to Buddhism, showcasing the deep historical roots of the location.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
Ashok Stambh, also known as The Lion Pillar, is a historical Buddhist excavation site located in Kolhua, Vaishali. Built by Emperor Ashoka, the 18.3m tall highly polished red sandstone pillar features a life-size lion statue and a bell-shaped capital. It stands beside a brick stupa and the Ramkund tank, commemorating Buddha's last sermon. The site is about 65 km from Patna and is well connected with nearby attractions like Abhishek Puskarni and Raja Vishal ka Garh. Facilities include parking, restaurants, and permitted photography.

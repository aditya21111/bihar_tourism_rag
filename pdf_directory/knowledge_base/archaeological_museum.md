OVERVIEW:
Nalanda Museum is one of the most prestigious site-museums of the Archaeological Survey of India. It houses antiquities found at the site of the ancient Nalanda Mahavihara, the earliest university cum monastery complex.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The museum displays about 350 ancient artifacts across four galleries and a main hall, while more than thirteen thousand artifacts are securely kept in the reserve collection. The rich display includes stone images and sculptures, bronzes, stuccos, terracottas, inscriptions, iron objects, ivory and bone objects, and potteries, offering an exceptional glimpse into the art and life of the Nalanda Mahavihara. The Mahavihara flourished between the 5th and 12th centuries under the patronage of the Gupta, Maukhari, and Pala rulers.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
It preserves the heritage of Nalanda Mahavihara, which flourished during the 5th – 12th centuries CE under prominent dynasties. The collection embodies the high point of Buddhist art and ancient Indian education.

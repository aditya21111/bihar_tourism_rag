OVERVIEW:
Shergarh Fort is a ruined hill fort located on the Kaimur plateau at about 800 feet in height. Fortified by Emperor Sher Shah Suri, the fort rests in a dense forest which makes access from the bottom of the plateau difficult. Known as Bhurkuda Qila in the 16th century, it is spread over an area of nearly 16 square kilometers.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Spread over an expansive 16 sq. km, the fort sits atop the Kaimur plateau bordering the Durgavati River. Its main entrance originally possessed eight small domes, though only five remain today. About one kilometre from the main entrance is a scenic lake known as Rani Pokhra (Queen's Pond). Additionally, an underground fort is located on an adjacent hill. 

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Built and fortified by Emperor Sher Shah Suri, demonstrating 16th-century fortification techniques. It held strategic importance due to its difficult access through the dense forests of the Kaimur plateau.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:

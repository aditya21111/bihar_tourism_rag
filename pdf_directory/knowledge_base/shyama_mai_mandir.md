OVERVIEW:
Shyama Mai Mandir is a unique and world-famous temple constructed atop a funeral site in Darbhanga, Bihar. Devoted to Goddess Kali, the temple was built over the funeral pyre of Maharaja Rameshwar of Darbhanga, a devout follower of the Goddess. Despite its construction over a funeral pyre, all religious functions and traditional ceremonies are actively performed here. The sanctum sanctorum houses a huge statue of Goddess Kali, where puja is conducted using both Vedic and Tantrik methods.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The Shyama Mai temple boasts a highly distinctive architecture and setting, primarily because it is constructed atop a funeral pyre, an arrangement that makes it world-renowned. The temple stands over the pyre of Maharaja Rameshwar of Darbhanga. Inside the sanctum sanctorum, visitors and devotees can view a massive statue of Goddess Kali. The temple follows fantastic customs, actively hosting all religious functions. The daily rituals and pujas are unique as they incorporate both Vedic and Tantrik traditions.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The temple holds substantial historical and cultural ties to the royal family of Darbhanga. It commemorates Maharaja Rameshwar of Darbhanga, built directly over his funeral pyre. The continuation of both Vedic and Tantrik worship methods at a funeral site presents a profound cultural anomaly that attracts widespread attention and reverence.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
It is said that this temple is constructed over the funeral pyre of Maharaja Rameshwar of Darbhanga, who was an ardent devotee of Goddess Kali. A fascinating tradition observed here is that despite being built on a funeral site, all standard religious functions and customs are continuously performed. Puja rituals are uniquely conducted by combining both Vedic and Tantrik methods.

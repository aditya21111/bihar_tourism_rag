OVERVIEW:
The Buddha Relic Stupa in Vaishali is a highly revered Buddhist site that enshrines one of the eight parts of the mortal remains of Lord Buddha after his Mahaparinirvana. This protected Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) site holds immense historical and spiritual significance.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located around 55km northwest of Patna in the Vaishali district, the Buddha Relic Stupa was originally built by the Lichhavis as a mud-stupa in the 5th century BC. Excavations at the core of the stupa yielded a relic casket containing the holy ashes of Lord Buddha mixed with earth. The casket also held a piece of conch, pieces of beads, a thin golden leaf, and a copper punch-marked coin. This makes it an incredibly sacred site for Buddhists worldwide and an important archaeological landmark.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Built by the Lichhavis in the 5th century BC, this mud-stupa is historically monumental. It is one of the original eight stupas built over the ashes of Lord Buddha, making it one of the holiest sites for Buddhist pilgrimage.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to tradition, after Lord Buddha attained Mahaparinirvana, his mortal remains were divided into eight parts. This stupa was built to enshrine the portion belonging to the Lichhavis of Vaishali.

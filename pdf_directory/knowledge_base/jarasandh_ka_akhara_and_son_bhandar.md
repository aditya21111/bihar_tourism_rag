OVERVIEW:
Jarasandh ka Akhara and Son Bhandar are two major historical sites located in Rajgir. Jarasandh ka Akhara traces its history back to the Mahabharata period and is believed to be the spot where King Jarasandh defeated 99 kings, intending to sacrifice them. The Son Bhandar Caves are two ancient, man-made caves carved into the base of the Vaibhar Hills, generally dating back to the 3rd or 4th century CE. Both sites present a rich glimpse into ancient Indian mythology and history.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The historical landscape of Rajgir includes the fascinating sites of Jarasandh ka Akhara and the Son Bhandar caves. Jarasandh ka Akhara is an ancient martial arts arena associated with the Mahabharata era. According to legendary accounts, it is the place where the powerful King Jarasandh imprisoned 99 rival kings after defeating them, intending to present them as sacrifices. Close by, the Son Bhandar Caves offer significant archaeological value. These are two distinct man-made caves that have been carefully carved into the rocky base of the Vaibhar Hills. Historians and archaeologists generally date the creation of the Son Bhandar caves to the 3rd or 4th century CE.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Jarasandh ka Akhara carries profound mythological significance, serving as a tangible link to the events described in the Mahabharata. The Son Bhandar caves represent early Indian rock-cut architecture, showcasing the craftsmanship and monastic or storage utility of the 3rd to 4th century CE.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to Mahabharata legends, Jarasandh ka Akhara is the exact location where King Jarasandh held 99 defeated kings captive with the intention of sacrificing them.

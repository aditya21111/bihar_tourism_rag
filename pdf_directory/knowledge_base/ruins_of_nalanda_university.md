OVERVIEW:
The Ruins of Nalanda University mark the site of the renowned mahavihara (Buddhist monastic university) in ancient Magadha, modern-day Bihar. Considered by historians to be the world's first residential university and among the greatest centers of learning in the ancient world, it operated from 427 until 1197 CE. Located near the ancient city of Rajagriha (Rajgir), Nalanda played a critical role in promoting arts and academics during India's "Golden Age" and was home to some of the most revered scholars of Mahayana Buddhism.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Nalanda Mahavihara, located about 90 kilometres southeast of Pataliputra (modern Patna) near Rajgir, was an epicenter of intellectual pursuit. Operating from 427 until 1197 CE, it is celebrated as the world's first residential university. During the 5th and 6th centuries, often termed the "Golden Age of India," the institution played a vital role in promoting academic excellence and the patronage of arts. For approximately 750 years, the university's faculty was composed of some of the most revered scholars of Mahayana Buddhism. The curriculum was vast, teaching six major Buddhist schools and philosophies including Yogachara and Sarvastivada, as well as secular subjects such as grammar, medicine, logic, and mathematics. The ruins stand today as a testament to this incredible legacy of ancient learning.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Nalanda University holds unparalleled historical and cultural significance. It was one of the greatest centers of learning in the ancient world and the world's first residential university. It was a crucial pillar of the "Golden Age of India," attracting scholars and students from across the globe and playing a fundamental role in the development and spread of Mahayana Buddhism and various secular sciences.

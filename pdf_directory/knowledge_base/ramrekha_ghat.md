OVERVIEW:
Ramrekha Ghat is an important religious place located in the Buxar district, uniquely associated with the Ramayana period. Situated on the banks of the river Ganges, mythology asserts that after Lord Shri Ram was accused of killing a woman (the demoness Tadka), he went to this Ganges bank to take a bath and cleanse himself of the sin. He made a Shivling out of clay to worship. It is said that his hand's line and his footprints were imprinted here during his prayers. The Shivling is still in existence at Ram Rekha Ghat, and devotees continue to come here to worship, reflecting profound feelings of devotion and reverence.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located on the serene banks of the river Ganges, Ramrekha Ghat holds deep mythological importance from the era of the Ramayana. The site features an earthen Shivling originally shaped by Lord Shri Ram. According to religious lore, the raw soil began flowing while being bathed, prompting Lord Ram to place his hand on it, leaving an imprint of his hand lines and footprints. Devotees visit this ghat to experience its powerful spiritual aura, offering prayers and finding peace in a location touched directly by divine legends.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The ghat holds tremendous cultural and religious significance as it is intimately linked to the Ramayana. It acts as an active place of reverence and devotion for pilgrims who come to view the ancient Shivling.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
The site's primary legend states that Lord Shri Ram came here to bathe in the Ganges to cleanse himself of the sin of killing the demoness Tadka. He created a Shivling out of clay to worship. While performing the ritual water bath, the raw soil started to flow. Lord Ram placed his hand on it to secure it, resulting in the imprint of his hand's lines on the Shivling and his footprints on the soil, which are still venerated today.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
Ramrekha Ghat is a sacred religious site situated on the banks of the Ganges in Buxar district, Bihar. Deeply linked to the Ramayana, it is believed to be the place where Lord Shri Ram bathed to cleanse the sin of slaying the demoness Tadka. The site is renowned for a clay Shivling that bears the imprinted hand lines of Lord Ram, along with his holy footprints. Devotees flock to this ancient site year-round, ideally between October and March. The ghat is easily accessible from Buxar Railway Station (2 km) and Buxar Bus Stand (1 km), and is nearby other attractions like the British Cemetery and Naulakha Mandir.

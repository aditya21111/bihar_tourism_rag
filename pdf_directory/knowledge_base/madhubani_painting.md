OVERVIEW:
Madhubani art, or Mithila painting, is a prominent style of Indian painting practiced in the Mithila region of the Indian subcontinent. Characterised by its eye-catching geometrical patterns, it is a significant cultural attraction deeply rooted in the rituals and festivals of the region.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
This traditional painting style is crafted using a variety of unconventional tools, including fingers, twigs, brushes, nib-pens, and matchsticks. The artworks are vibrant, created using natural dyes and pigments. The distinguishing features of Madhubani paintings are the intricate and eye-catching geometrical patterns that convey deep cultural meanings and traditional aesthetics.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Madhubani painting holds profound ritual significance in the Mithila region. It is traditionally created for particular life occasions, such as birth or marriage, and to celebrate major religious festivals. 

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
The art form is intrinsically linked to local traditions and is a ritualistic practice for major life events (birth, marriage) and festivals like Holi, Surya Shasti, Kali Puja, Upanayana, and Durga Puja.

* Rich ritual content associated with life events and festivals.

Madhubani painting, also known as Mithila painting, is a traditional Indian art form originating from the Mithila region. Known for striking geometric patterns, it is created using natural dyes and tools like fingers, twigs, and matchsticks.

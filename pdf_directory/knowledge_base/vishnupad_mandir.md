OVERVIEW:
Vishnupad Mandir is an ancient Hindu temple dedicated to Lord Vishnu, located on the banks of the Phalgu river in Gaya. It is highly revered as the central site for Shradha rites in Hindu tradition. The temple is built over the legendary site where Lord Vishnu purportedly killed or pinned the demon Gayasura underground, leaving behind a 40-cm footprint incised into a basalt block called Dharmasila.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located prominently along the Phalgu river, the Vishnupad temple's central attraction is a 40-cm footprint purported to be of Lord Vishnu. The footprint is incised into a block of basalt known as Dharmasila. The temple is crowned by a striking 50-kilo gold flag, which was donated by a devoted follower, Gayapal Panda Bal Govind Sen. Over the centuries, the shrine has been visited by many legendary saints, including Madhvacharya, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and Vallabhacharya.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The temple is a cornerstone of Hindu religious practices, serving as the primary center for Shradha rites in Gaya. The visits from eminent saints like Madhvacharya, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and Vallabhacharya further highlight its historical and spiritual stature.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to Hindu mythology, Lord Vishnu killed the demon Gayasura by stepping on his chest and pinning him underground. The Dharmasila basalt block at the temple retains the 40-cm footprint left by Lord Vishnu during this act. It is also the traditional epicenter for performing Shradha rites to honor ancestors.

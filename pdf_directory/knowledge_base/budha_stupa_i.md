OVERVIEW:
Budha Stupa-I is a significant archaeological and religious site located in the ancient region of Vaishali. The stupa is historically crucial as it enshrined a part of the sacred ashes of Lord Buddha in a stone casket. It stands as a monument of veneration in an area that played a pivotal role in early Buddhism and ancient democratic history.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The exterior of Budha Stupa-I has a plain surface. Its primary religious and historical value lies within, as it was constructed to house a part of the sacred ashes of Lord Buddha, enshrined here in a stone casket following his Mahaparinirvana.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Vaishali is renowned as one of the first democratic republics in the world, thriving in the 6th century BCE under the Lichchavis and Vajjis. Lord Buddha visited Vaishali many times and preached his last sermon nearby at Kolhua. This stupa holds profound cultural and religious significance as a reliquary for the Buddha's ashes.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
The region was a stronghold of the Lichhavis, who were avid followers of the Buddha. The stupa commemorates the deep connection between Buddha and the people of Vaishali.

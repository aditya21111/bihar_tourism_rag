OVERVIEW:
Rajnagar, the nearest village to Madhubani in Bihar, is home to the ruins of the grand Rajnagar Palace, which was severely destroyed in the devastating 1934 earthquake. The site was developed by Maharajadhiraj Sir Rameshwar Singh, who intended to shift the headquarters of the Raj Darbhanga from Darbhanga to Rajnagar. He invested heavily in constructing magnificent palaces, ornate temples, and excavating or renovating large tanks. Unfortunately, the earthquake reduced the palaces almost beyond repair and badly damaged the temples, turning the main palace (including the Durga hall and the roof over the inner shrines) into a mass of ruins and shattering the marble image of the deity into pieces.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The Rajnagar Fort and Palace complex once stood as a symbol of immense wealth and architectural ambition under the Raj Darbhanga. Envisioned by Maharajadhiraj Sir Rameshwar Singh as the new headquarters for his estate, the complex featured an array of majestic palaces, beautifully crafted temples, and numerous renovated water tanks. Today, visitors can explore the poignant ruins left behind by the tragic 1934 earthquake. The remnants of the main palace, the Durga hall, and the inner shrines showcase glimpses of its former glory, although the structures were rendered into a mass of ruins and the delicate marble deity images were broken. It remains a fascinating site for history enthusiasts and architecture lovers.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The site is highly significant as a testament to the grandeur of the Raj Darbhanga estate under Maharajadhiraj Sir Rameshwar Singh and serves as a historical reminder of the catastrophic impact of the 1934 earthquake.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
Rajnagar Fort, located in Rajnagar village near Madhubani, Bihar, features the dramatic ruins of the Rajnagar Palace. Built by Maharajadhiraj Sir Rameshwar Singh to serve as the new headquarters for the Raj Darbhanga estate, the complex once included grand palaces, temples, and tanks. It was tragically destroyed in the massive 1934 earthquake, which left the main palace, Durga hall, and inner shrines in ruins and shattered its marble deities. Open from 6:00 AM to 9:00 PM and best visited from October to March, the site is accessible from Darbhanga Airport (46 Kms) and Madhubani Railway Station (13 Kms). Nearby attractions include Kapileshwar Sthan Temple and Ugna Mahadev Temple.

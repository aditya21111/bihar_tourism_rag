OVERVIEW:
Kumhrar Park is located in the area of Patna where remains of the ancient city of Pataliputra were excavated by the Archaeological Survey of India. The site features archaeological remains of the Mauryan period (322–185 BCE), including the ruins of a hypostyle 80-pillared hall. Excavation findings date back to 600 BCE and mark the ancient capital of Ajatsatru, Chandragupta, and Ashoka. The relics range from four continuous periods from 600 BCE to 600 CE. 

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Kumhrar Park contains the archaeological ruins of Pataliputra, showcasing elements from the Mauryan period. Its most famous feature is the ruins of a hypostyle 80-pillared hall. Currently, there is one pillar of polished stone and a large number of fragments. The excavators were able to trace 72 'pits' of ash and rubble on the site, which marked the position where other pillars once stood. 

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The site is of immense historical and archaeological significance as it marks the ancient capital of Pataliputra, ruled by prominent emperors like Ajatsatru, Chandragupta, and Ashoka. The relics span four continuous periods from 600 BCE to 600 CE, specifically showcasing the Mauryan architecture from 322–185 BCE.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
Kumhrar Park, located in Patna, is an important archaeological site containing the excavated remains of the ancient city of Pataliputra. Notably featuring the ruins of a Mauryan period (322–185 BCE) 80-pillared hall, it includes one polished stone pillar and trace pits of 72 others. Relics span from 600 BCE to 600 CE, marking the capital of emperors like Ajatsatru, Chandragupta, and Ashoka. The park is best visited between October and March from 10:00 AM to 04:00 PM with an entry fee of Rs. 25. The site offers parking and restaurants, and is 5 Kms from Rajendra Nagar Railway Station.

OVERVIEW:
Bihar Museum is located in Patna, planned as a history museum for the state of Bihar on Bailey Road (Nehru Path). The museum's initial opening included the children's museum, the main entrance area, and an orientation theatre. Later, remaining galleries were opened. More than 100 artifacts were transferred here from the Patna Museum. Among the highlight exhibits is the 2300-year-old Didarganj Yakshi, one of the finest sandstone statues in the world, showing the Yakshi with a fly-whisk (chauri) in her right hand and a broken left hand. The museum is spread over 5.6 hectares with 24,000 square meters of built area. The permanent galleries display artifacts from ancient India to 1764, while post-1764 artifacts are kept at the Patna Museum.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The Bihar Museum is a massive state-of-the-art history museum spread over a sprawling 5.6 hectares campus. It has 24,000 square meters of built area featuring nine permanent galleries. It was developed to showcase the rich heritage and history of Bihar up to the year 1764. The complex includes an orientation theatre and a dedicated children's museum. The most iconic artifact housed here is the 2300-year-old Didarganj Yakshi, a masterpiece of ancient Indian sandstone carving.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
It serves as the primary repository for Bihar's ancient historical artifacts, taking over pre-1764 collections from the older Patna Museum. The museum preserves thousands of years of the state's vibrant cultural and archaeological history.

Spanning 5.6 hectares, it displays artifacts from ancient India up to 1764, most notably the 2300-year-old Didarganj Yakshi sandstone statue.

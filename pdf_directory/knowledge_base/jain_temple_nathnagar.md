OVERVIEW:
The Champapur Digambar Jain Mandir in Nathnagar, Bhagalpur, is an ancient and historical temple dedicated to Vasupujya, the 12th Tirthankara. Champapur holds massive religious significance for Jains as it is believed that all the five "Kalyanakas" of Vasupujya Tirthankara took place here. The main temple is believed to be around 2500 years old.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Champapur is historically significant, said to have been the capital of the Anga Kingdom ruled by Karna, the sixth Pandava. The ancient Main temple of Champapur is roughly 2500 years old and is adorned with five altars that symbolize the Five Kalyanaka. The temple compound once featured four spectacular 'Columns of Fame' at each corner, of which two have survived and can be seen today.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The site is profoundly sacred in Jainism, marking the location where the five "Kalyanakas" of the 12th Tirthankara, Vasupujya, occurred. Historically, Champapur was the capital of the Anga Kingdom under Karna from the Mahabharata era.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to belief, this is the site of the five "Kalyanakas" of Vasupujya Tirthankara. It is also linked to the Mahabharata epic as the ancient capital of Karna's Anga Kingdom.

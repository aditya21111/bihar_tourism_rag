OVERVIEW:
The Champapur Digambar Jain Mandir is an ancient and historical Jain temple located in Nathnagar, Bhagalpur. Dedicated to Vasupujya, the 12th Tirthankara, this site is of immense religious significance. The main temple of Champapur is believed to be about 2500 years old, adorned with altars and surviving 'Columns of Fame', making it a prime destination for Jain pilgrims and history enthusiasts.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The Champapur Digambar Jain Mandir is dedicated to Vasupujya, the 12th Tirthankara. The main temple is believed to be approximately 2500 years old and is adorned with five altars symbolizing the Five Kalyanaka. The compound also features two spectacular 'Columns of Fame' that have survived among the four that originally existed on each corner of the compound.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The temple is highly historical. Champapur is said to have been the capital of the Anga Kingdom, which was ruled by Karna, the sixth Pandava from the Mahabharata. It holds unique religious importance for Jains, as it is believed that all five "Kalyanakas" of Vasupujya Tirthankara took place in Champapur.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
It is a traditional belief that the city of Champapur was the capital of the Anga Kingdom ruled by Karna. It is also believed that all five significant life events (Kalyanakas) of the 12th Tirthankara, Vasupujya, occurred here.

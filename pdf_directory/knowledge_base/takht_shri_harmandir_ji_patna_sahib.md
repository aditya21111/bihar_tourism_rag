OVERVIEW:
Takht Sri Patna Sahib, located in Patna City, is one of the five Takhts of the Sikhs. It was built to commemorate the birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru, who was born here on 22 December 1666. He spent his early years here before moving to Anandpur Sahib. Patna was also honored by visits from Guru Nanak and Guru Tegh Bahadur. The construction of the Takht was initiated by Maharaja Ranjit Singh in the 18th century. Following damage caused by an earthquake in 1934, the building was rebuilt between 1948 and 1957.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Takht Shri Harmandir Ji Patna Sahib stands as a grand Gurudwara marking the birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh. The architectural marvel was originally constructed by Maharaja Ranjit Singh in the 18th century and later rebuilt after the 1934 earthquake. It stands as a pivotal pilgrimage destination for Sikhs globally, bearing deep historical ties to multiple Sikh Gurus.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
It holds immense religious importance as the birthplace of the tenth Sikh Guru, Guru Gobind Singh, and is one of the five holy Takhts of Sikhism. The site is also historically significant due to the visits of Guru Nanak and Guru Tegh Bahadur.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
The site is revered for having housed Guru Gobind Singh during his early years. It is a central place for Sikh worship, emphasizing equality, community, and devotion.

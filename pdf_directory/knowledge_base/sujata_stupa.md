OVERVIEW:
Sujata Stupa, also known as Sujata Garh, is an ancient Buddhist stupa located in the village of Senanigrama (Bakraur), lying directly across the Phalgu River from Bodh Gaya. Built initially in the 2nd century BCE, the stupa is dedicated to the milkmaid Sujata, who offered Gautama Buddha milk and rice, ending his seven years of fasting and asceticism and helping him attain illumination.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located just a 20-minute walk from Bodh Gaya across the Phalgu River, Sujata Stupa is a prominent Buddhist historical site. It was initially constructed in the 2nd century BCE, a date confirmed by the discovery of Dark Grey polished wares and a punch-marked coin in the adjacent monastery. The stupa serves as a monument to Sujata, the milkmaid from the village of Bakraur who played a pivotal role in the Buddha's journey to enlightenment.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The site has immense historical and archaeological importance. Dating back to the 2nd century BCE, the stupa's antiquity is verified by archaeological finds such as Dark Grey polished wares and punch-marked coins. It memorializes the critical moment when Buddha abandoned extreme asceticism for the Middle Path.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to tradition, the stupa is dedicated to a milkmaid named Sujata from Bakraur. It is believed that at this very spot, while Gautama Buddha was sitting under a Banyan tree, Sujata fed him milk and rice. This meal ended his severe seven-year fasting and asceticism, ultimately allowing him to attain illumination through the Middle Path.

CATEGORY:
Historical Site / Heritage Site

OVERVIEW:
Rohtasgarh Fort is one of the largest forts in the world, spreading over 42 sq. km. It remains remarkably intact and was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997. UNESCO describes it as an exceptional example of the Muslim military architecture of South Asia.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Sprawling across 42 square kilometers, the fort has 83 gates and a vast network of underground places and tunnels. Entrances are heavily guarded; a gate with a cupola is the first of many well-guarded entrances. The main gate is known as Hathiya Pol or the Elephant Gate, named after the figures of elephants upon it. Notable structures inside the fort include Aina Mahal, Jamia Masjid, Habsh Khan's mausoleum, Ganesh Temple, Rohtasan Temple, and Devi Temple.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The fort is an exceptional piece of Muslim military architecture in South Asia and has maintained remarkable structural integrity. It was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997.

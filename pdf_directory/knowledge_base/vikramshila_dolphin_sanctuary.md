OVERVIEW:
Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary is located in Bhagalpur District of Bihar, India. It is a 60 kilometers stretch of the Ganges River from Sultanganj to Kahalgaon. Notified as a protected area for the endangered Gangetic dolphins in Asia in 1991, it is a crucial habitat where half of the remaining few hundred dolphins are found. The Gangetic Dolphin has been declared as the national aquatic animal of India.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The sanctuary encompasses a 60-kilometer stretch of the Ganges River flowing between Sultanganj and Kahalgaon in the Bhagalpur district. It serves as a vital protected area dedicated to the conservation of the endangered Gangetic dolphins in Asia. Despite once being found in abundance, only a few hundred remain today, with half of this surviving population residing in this specific stretch.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Notified as the Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary in 1991. The Gangetic Dolphin holds significant national importance, having been declared as the national aquatic animal of India.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
Vikramshila Dolphin Sanctuary (also known as Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary) is a major wildlife area and natural attraction located along a 60 km stretch of the Ganges River from Sultanganj to Kahalgaon in Bhagalpur District, Bihar. Notified in 1991, it is a key protected area for the endangered Gangetic dolphin, the national aquatic animal of India. Half of the surviving population of these dolphins resides here. The best time to visit is from October to March between 05:00 AM and 05:00 PM. The sanctuary is accessible via Patna Airport (232 km) and Vikramshila Railway Station (4 km), and offers facilities such as parking, restaurants, drinking water, and a souvenir shop. Ajgaibinath Temple is a nearby attraction.

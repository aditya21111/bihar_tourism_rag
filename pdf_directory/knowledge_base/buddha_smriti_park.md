OVERVIEW:
Buddha Smriti Park is a 22-acre urban park located in the heart of Patna, developed to commemorate the 2554th birth anniversary of Lord Buddha. Inaugurated by the 14th Dalai Lama, the park features two sacred Bodhi tree saplings planted by him—one brought from Bodh Gaya and the other from Anuradhapura in Sri Lanka. The original Mahabodhi tree branch is believed to have been taken to Anuradhapura by Emperor Ashoka's son, Mahendra. The central attraction of the park is the Patliputra Karuna Stupa, a 200-foot high stupa situated in the middle, housing the pot containing the holy ashes of Buddha. The park also includes a museum where Buddhist relics from Japan, Myanmar, South Korea, Sri Lanka, and Thailand are installed.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located centrally near Patna Junction, Buddha Smriti Park is a sprawling 22-acre urban park built to honor the 2554th birth anniversary of Buddha. The park's main feature is the towering 200-foot Patliputra Karuna Stupa which houses a pot containing the holy ashes of Lord Buddha. The park is deeply serene, offering visitors the chance to see sacred Bodhi tree saplings planted by the Dalai Lama. Additionally, there is a dedicated museum showcasing Buddhist relics brought from various Southeast Asian and East Asian countries.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
It holds immense religious and cultural importance as it houses the holy ashes of Lord Buddha and sacred Bodhi tree saplings. It acts as a spiritual and cultural center commemorating the 2554th birth anniversary of Buddha.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
A branch of the original Mahabodhi tree at Bodh Gaya is believed to have been taken to Anuradhapura in Sri Lanka by Emperor Ashoka's son, Mahendra. A sapling from that very tree in Anuradhapura was brought back and planted here by the 14th Dalai Lama.

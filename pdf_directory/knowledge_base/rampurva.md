OVERVIEW:
Rampurva is a significant historical and archaeological site located in West Champaran, near the River Harbora (ancient Anoma). It is renowned for the discovery of two huge Ashoka pillars. The site holds immense religious and historical importance as the legendary place where Lord Buddha embraced the life of an ascetic to seek the truth about human suffering. 

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The site is marked by two massive Ashoka pillars excavated from the area. One of these pillars features six edicts etched onto its surface, with that particular side preserved under a concrete canopy. This pillar also bears engravings of a peacock and a bird. Originally, the pillars featured exquisite capitals: a Lion Capital, which is now preserved at the Indian Museum in Kolkata, and a magnificent Bull Capital, which currently graces the Rashtrapati Bhawan in New Delhi.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Emperor Ashoka constructed these pillars to monumentalize a crucial turning point in Lord Buddha's life—his transition from a royal prince to a wandering ascetic.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to legend, after leaving his family and kingdom behind, Lord Buddha reached the banks of the Anoma River (now Harbora). Here, he changed out of his royal garments, adopted a sadhu's attire, and cut his hair locks, officially beginning his spiritual journey to find the cause of suffering for all living beings.

KEY HIGHLIGHTS:
* Two excavated Ashoka pillars.
* Six edicts etched on one of the pillars.
* Engravings of a peacock and a bird.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
Rampurva, located in West Champaran near the River Harbora (ancient Anoma), is a major Buddhist historical site featuring two excavated Ashoka pillars. It marks the legendary spot where Lord Buddha discarded his royal attire to become an ascetic. Key highlights include six Ashokan edicts, a preserved Lion Capital (now in Kolkata), and a Bull Capital (now in New Delhi). It is accessible via Patna Airport and Bettiah/Narkatiaganj railway stations.

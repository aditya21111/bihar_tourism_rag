OVERVIEW:
Xuanzang Memorial or Hiuen Tsang Memorial Hall in Nalanda marks the place where the renowned 7th-century Chinese Buddhist monk, scholar, traveler, and translator Xuanzang ended his long pilgrimage in pursuit of the true understanding of Buddhism.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The memorial is dedicated to Hiuen Tsang, who is celebrated for his travelogue covering his journey to India between 629–645 CE, his massive efforts to bring over 657 Indian texts back to China, and his subsequent translations of these texts, which heavily influenced Chinese Buddhism. The construction of the Memorial was a symbol of cross-cultural friendship, initiated jointly in 1957 by the first Prime Minister of India and the first Premier of China.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The site is of immense cultural and historical importance, commemorating Hiuen Tsang's contributions to Chinese Buddhism and Sino-Indian relations. It represents his quest for knowledge that led him to the great ancient university of Nalanda.

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
The Hiuen Tsang Memorial Hall (Xuanzang Memorial) in Nalanda celebrates the 7th-century Chinese monk who traveled to India to study Buddhism. Initiated in 1957 by Indian and Chinese leaders, it commemorates his travelogue and efforts to take 657 Indian texts to China. Located near Nalanda University ruins, it is open from October to March (09:00AM - 05:00PM) with entry fees of Rs. 30 (Domestic) and Rs. 200 (Foreign). It offers parking, a restaurant, and a souvenir shop.

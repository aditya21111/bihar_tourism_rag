OVERVIEW:
Buddha Smriti Park is a 22-acre urban park located in the heart of Patna, developed to commemorate the 2554th birth anniversary of Buddha. Inaugurated by the 14th Dalai Lama, the park features two sacred Bodhi tree saplings and a 200-foot-high central stupa known as Patliputra Karuna Stupa. It also houses holy ashes of Buddha and a museum showcasing Buddhist relics from several Asian countries.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located in the heart of Patna city, Buddha Smriti Park is an urban park spanning 22 acres. It was developed specifically to commemorate the 2554th birth anniversary of Gautama Buddha. The park was inaugurated by the 14th Dalai Lama, who planted two saplings of the sacred Bodhi tree here. One sapling was brought from Bodh Gaya and the other from Anuradhapura in Sri Lanka. The central attraction of the park is the towering Patliputra Karuna Stupa, which is 200 feet high. Inside this main stupa is a pot containing the holy ashes of Buddha. Additionally, the park features a museum where Buddhist relics imported from Japan, Myanmar, South Korea, Sri Lanka, and Thailand are exhibited.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The park stands as a significant cultural and religious monument dedicated to Buddhism. It houses the holy ashes of Buddha and sacred Bodhi tree saplings, emphasizing its deep spiritual connections to Bodh Gaya and Anuradhapura. 

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
A branch of the original Mahabodhi tree at Bodh Gaya is believed to have been taken to Anuradhapura in Sri Lanka by Emperor Ashoka's son Mahendra. The saplings planted in this park are derived from these sacred lineages.

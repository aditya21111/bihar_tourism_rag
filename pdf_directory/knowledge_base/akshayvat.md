OVERVIEW:
Akshayvat, meaning the everlasting and indestructible "Banyan Tree", is a profoundly sacred site in Maranpur, Gaya. The tree holds immense religious significance, especially for the Pinda Daan rituals, which commence at the Falgu River and conclude here. According to Hindu mythology, the tree received a boon from Sita rendering it immortal. Additionally, the Ved Puran states that Lord Krishna resides under its shadow, and it will survive even when the entire world is submerged in water.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Akshayvat is a massive, indestructible banyan tree situated in Maranpur, Gaya. It marks the culmination point of the sacred Pinda Daan rituals that begin at the Falgu River. This location is central to Hindu pilgrimage in Gaya, revered due to its eternal nature granted by a mythological boon. The area around the tree serves as a major gathering point for devotees performing rites for their ancestors.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The tree is of paramount importance in the performance of Pinda Daan, the Hindu ritual of paying homage to deceased ancestors. Its enduring presence has made it a focal point for pilgrims for centuries.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to legend, the tree received a boon from Sita, making it immortal and incapable of dying. The Ved Puran states that Lord Krishna stays under its shadow, and when the whole world ultimately drowns in water, this tree will survive due to Sita's boon. It marks the endpoint of the Pinda Daan ritual that begins at the Falgu River.

OVERVIEW:
Agam Kuan is an ancient well and archaeological site in Patna, dating back to the period of Mauryan emperor Ashoka. Circular in shape, the well is lined with brick in the upper 13 metres (43 ft) and contains wooden rings in the remaining 19 metres (62 ft). Adjacent to it is the Shitala Devi temple, where the folk deity statue of Shitala Devi is located. Inside this temple, the pindas of the Saptamatrikas (the seven mother goddesses) are worshipped. The temple is widely revered for its belief in curing smallpox and chicken pox.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The site encompasses both the historical Agam Kuan well and the revered Sheetla Mandir. The ancient well, Agam Kuan, is circular and dates back to the Mauryan period under Emperor Ashoka. It measures 13 metres lined with brick and an additional 19 metres with wooden rings. The Shitala Devi temple houses the statue of Shitala Devi and the pindas of the Saptamatrikas. It holds strong religious significance for locals.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Agam Kuan is a significant archaeological feature from the reign of Emperor Ashoka. The attached Shitala Devi temple is deeply culturally significant as a center of faith and traditional healing. 

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
The temple is widely revered for its belief in curing smallpox and chicken pox. Devotees visit the temple to seek the blessings of Shitala Devi for health and recovery from these illnesses. Inside the temple, the Saptamatrikas (the seven mother goddesses) are worshipped.

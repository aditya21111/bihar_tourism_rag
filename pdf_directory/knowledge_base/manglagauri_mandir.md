OVERVIEW:
Mangla Gauri temple is a prominent Shakti Peetham situated on Mangalagauri hill in Gaya, Bihar. Dating back to the 15th century, the temple is dedicated to Sati, or the Mother Goddess, who is worshipped here as the Goddess of benevolence. Although Gaya is predominantly a Vaishnavite pilgrimage center, this temple stands out as one of the eighteen maha shaktipeeths and specifically as an Upa-Shakti Peeth, where a part of Sati's body is believed to have fallen. The temple holds significant scriptural importance, being mentioned in several Puranas and Tantric works.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The present 15th-century east-facing temple is built atop the Mangalagauri hill and can be accessed via a flight of steps or a motorable road. The main sanctum houses the symbol of the Goddess, alongside finely carved ancient relief sculptures. Within the temple complex, there are also two minor shrines dedicated to Lord Shiva, as well as images of Mahishasura Mardini, Durga, and Dakshina Kali.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The temple is historically and culturally monumental. It is mentioned in revered texts such as the Padma Purana, Vayu Purana, Agni Purana, Devi Bhagvata Purana, and Markandeya Purana, as well as in other scriptures and tantric texts. It serves as a vital center of Goddess worship in a predominantly Vaishnavite area.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to Hindu mythology, the temple represents an Upa-Shakti Peeth, marking the spot where a part of Sati’s body fell. Mangalagauri is worshipped here specifically as the Goddess of benevolence.

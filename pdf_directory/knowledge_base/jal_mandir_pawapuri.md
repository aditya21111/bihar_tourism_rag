OVERVIEW:
The Jal Mandir or Water Temple is a highly revered major pilgrimage destination for Jains located in Pawapuri, which translates to "a town without sins." The magnificent temple is dedicated to Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, marking the sacred place of his cremation and where he attained Nirvana in 527 BC. 

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Jal Mandir is a picturesque white marble temple situated within a serene pond filled with red-coloured lotus flowers. The temple is one of the five main temples in Pawapuri and serves as the deified home of the "Charan Paduka" or foot impressions of Lord Mahavira. It was originally built by King Nandivardhan, the elder brother of Mahavira, to mark the exact spot where Mahavira was cremated.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Pawapuri holds profound religious and historical importance for Jains, as it is the place where Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, attained Nirvana (death) in 527 BC. The temple contains the sacred "Charan Paduka" (foot impression) of Mahavira. 

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to traditions, the location of the Jal Mandir was originally the site of Mahavira's cremation in 527 BC. The temple was built by King Nandivardhan, his elder brother. The name Pawapuri itself signifies "a town without sins."

RETRIEVAL SUMMARY:
Jal Mandir, also known as the Water Temple, is a major Jain religious site located in Pawapuri, Nalanda, Bihar. It marks the cremation site where Lord Mahavira attained Nirvana in 527 BC. Originally built by Mahavira's elder brother, King Nandivardhan, the temple stands in a pond of red lotuses and houses Mahavira's "Charan Paduka." The temple is located 88 Kms from Patna Airport and 10 Kms from Pawapuri Railway Station. Facilities include parking and restaurants. The best time to visit is from October to March.

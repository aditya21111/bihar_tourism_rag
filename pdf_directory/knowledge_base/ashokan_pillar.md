OVERVIEW:
The Ashokan Pillar is an ancient and historical monument situated in Lauriya Nandangarh, West Champaran, Bihar. Located near the banks of the Burhi Gandak River, the village derives its name from this very pillar, known locally as "laur". The site is also famous for the Nandangarh stupa, which dates back to the 3rd century BCE, providing immense archaeological and historical value.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Lauriya Nandangarh features an impressive Ashokan standing pillar ("laur"). Adjacent to it is the Nandangarh stupa complex which has 15 Stupa mounds arranged in three rows. Each row extends upwards of 600 meters. The first row begins near the Ashokan pillar and proceeds from East to West, while the other two rows are situated at right angles to the first and parallel to each other. The stupas date back to the 3rd century BCE.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The pillar is an important relic from Emperor Ashoka's era. The presence of the 3rd century BCE Nandangarh stupa mounds indicates that this area was an important religious and cultural center in ancient India. 

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
The village Lauriya Nandangarh draws its name directly from the Ashokan pillar, which is locally referred to as "laur".

OVERVIEW:
Akshyavat, located in Maranpur, Gaya, means "everlasting, indestructible Banyan Tree". It is a highly revered site where Pind Daan (offerings to ancestors) is performed. The Pinda Daan ritual starts from the Falgu River and finishes at Akshay Vat. According to belief, this tree received a boon from Sita that it can never die.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Akshyavat is a sacred and immortal banyan tree in Gaya. It holds immense importance for Hindu pilgrims performing Pind Daan rituals for their ancestors. The ritual traditionally concludes near this tree. The tree is considered indestructible and is steeped in mythological significance relating to Sita and Lord Krishna.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
It holds supreme importance in Hindu rituals, specifically the Pind Daan ceremony. It is deeply connected to Hindu epics and Puranic literature.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to legends, the tree received a boon from Sita making it immortal, which is why it is called "Akshayvat" (the indestructible Banyan tree). According to the VedPuran, Lord Krishna stays under its shadow, and when the whole world drowns in water during dissolution, this tree will not die due to Sita's boon. Pinda Daan rituals start from Falgu River and conclude here.

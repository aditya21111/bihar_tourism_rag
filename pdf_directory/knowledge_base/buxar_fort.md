OVERVIEW:
Buxar Fort is located on the banks of the river Ganga in Buxar, Bihar. The fort is believed to have been built by King Rudra Deo of the Kashi Naresh dynasty in the 11th century. The Buxar Fort has played a significant role in many historical battles, including the battles of Sher Shah Suri and Humayun in 1539, and the famous Battle of Buxar in 1764. The archaeological structure of this fort defines its glorious past and highlights the rich cultural heritage of the city. 

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located on the banks of the river Ganga, Buxar Fort is an impressive archaeological structure that stands as a testament to the glorious history of the region. Originally built in the 11th century, the fort has been a crucial military and strategic outpost, witnessing monumental historical clashes. In addition to the fort, the city of Buxar itself is deeply entrenched in cultural and mythological heritage, historically recognized as the site where Lord Rama killed the demon Taraka and where Sage Vishvamaitra's Ashram was located.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Built by King Rudra Deo of the Kashi Naresh dynasty in the 11th century. It holds immense historical significance due to its involvement in major Indian battles, notably the battle between Sher Shah Suri and Humayun in 1539, and the Battle of Buxar in 1764. Furthermore, according to mythology, the ancient significance of Buxar is mentioned in epics like Brahamana Purana and Varah Purana.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to mythology, Buxar is the place where Lord Rama killed the demon Taraka. It is also the site of Sage Vishvamaitra's Ashram. The word Buxar is said to be derived from "VyaghraSar," which means "the tiger face."

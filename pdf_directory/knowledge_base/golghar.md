OVERVIEW:
Golghar is a massive, historic granary located to the west of the Gandhi Maidan in Patna. Built in 1786, the unique beehive-shaped structure was intended to prevent famines by serving as a colossal grain store. Today, it stands as a significant architectural marvel and a major tourist attraction, offering visitors a panoramic view of Patna city and the river Ganges from its top.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The Golghar is a pillar-less structure with a distinct beehive shape, reaching a height of 29 meters. It boasts exceptionally thick walls, measuring 3.6 meters at the base, to support the massive volume of grain it was intended to hold. The architecture includes a 145-step spiral stairway that wraps around the exterior. This spiral staircase was cleverly designed to facilitate the passage of workers: they would ascend one side to deliver grain-bags through a hole at the top, and descend the other stairs to maintain a continuous flow. From the top, the Golghar presents a breathtaking panoramic view of the city and the Ganges.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Built by the British East India Company, the Golghar was intended to be the first of a series of huge grain stores following severe famines, although no others were ever built. The granary was designed by John Garstin of the Bengal Engineers, part of the Bengal Army. Its construction was completed on 20 July 1786. It remains a stark reminder of colonial famine prevention strategies and historical engineering.

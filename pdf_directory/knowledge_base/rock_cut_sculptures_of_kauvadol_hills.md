Historical Site / Archaeological Site

OVERVIEW:
The Rock Cut Sculptures of Kauvadol Hills form an important archaeological site in the sacred city of Gaya. Located near the Barabar Caves, the site features a huge rock balanced on a hillock and a magnificent excavated sitting Buddha statue, along with the remains of an ancient monastery used for training young Buddhist monks.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The site is distinguished by a huge rock balanced perfectly on a hillock. Excavations here revealed a magnificent sitting Buddha statue carved from black stone, measuring about 8 ft tall. At the base of the Kauvadol Hills lie the remains of an ancient monastery from the 4th century BCE, which served as a training school for young Buddhist monks, known as "shamner".

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The 4th century BCE monastery highlights the site's historical role in Buddhist monastic education. Its importance is further corroborated by the famous Chinese Traveller Xuanzang, who visited this place in the 7th century CE.

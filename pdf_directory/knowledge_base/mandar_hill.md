OVERVIEW:
Mandar Hill is a 700 ft high granite hill located in Banka, steeped in Hindu mythology. It is famous for its association with the legendary churning of the ocean (Amrita-Manthana) and also hosts two important Jain temples at its summit.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Situated about 48 km south of Bhagalpur town, Mandar Hill is a massive granite structure covered in low jungle near its summit, rising to a height of 700 feet. The hill is profoundly significant in Hindu mythology, identified in the Skand Purana as the Mandara or Sumeru Mountain. It was famously used by Gods and Demons as the churning stick during the 'Amrita-Manthana' (churning of the ocean) to extract the nectar of immortality (Amrit), using the serpent Vasuki as the rope. Beyond Hindu mythology, the hill is also a sacred pilgrimage site for Jains, featuring two Jain temples on its summit dedicated to the worship of Lord Vasupujyanatha.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
It holds immense mythological significance in Hinduism as the churning stick in the 'Amrita-Manthana' story. Simultaneously, it is a significant Jain pilgrimage site with temples dedicated to Lord Vasupujyanatha on its summit.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to the Skand Purana, Gods and Demons used this hill as a churning stick, with the mythical serpent Vasuki as a rope, to churn the ocean and obtain 'Amrit' (nectar of immortality).

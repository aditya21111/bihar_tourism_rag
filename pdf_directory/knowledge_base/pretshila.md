OVERVIEW:
Pretshila is a sacred mountain located 8 kms on the north western side of Gaya. It is a highly significant pilgrimage site for devotees performing Pinddaan to seek solace and Moksh (salvation) for their departed souls. The site features a temple dedicated to Lord Yam, the god of death, and a holy lake named Ramsagar.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Situated 8 kms north west of Gaya, Pretshila, also known as Korma Pahaar, is a prominent mountain site deeply connected to Hindu ancestral rituals. On top of the mountain sits a unique temple exclusively devoted to Lord Yam, the Hindu god of death. The site also encompasses a lake named Ramsagar, enhancing its spiritual ambiance.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Pretshila is crucial to the Pinddaan ritual in Gaya. It holds profound cultural and religious significance as it is said that the Pinddaan process is incomplete without offering rites at Pretshila. Doing so is believed to grant Moksh to the soul of the departed.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
According to tradition, devotees offer pinddaan at Pretshila for the solace of departed souls, believing that this final offering is necessary for the soul to achieve Moksh. Additionally, the adjacent lake named Ramsagar is associated with Lord Ram, who is said to have taken a bath there.

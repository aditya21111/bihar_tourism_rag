OVERVIEW:
The Ruins of Vikramshila University form an incredible historical site in Bhagalpur, representing one of ancient India's most significant centers of learning. Founded by the Pala King Dharmapala, the university thrived between the 9th to 13th centuries CE as a bustling education center for a large number of teachers and students.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Excavations at the site have unearthed massive and structurally complex ruins. At the center lies a colossal square remnant of a double-storeyed stupa featuring a cruciform structure. The site also includes the remains of an ancient library and an array of votive stupas. In addition to Buddhist structures, a Tibetan and a Hindu temple have also been discovered. During its peak, the university taught a wide variety of subjects including Philosophy, Theology, Metaphysics, Grammar, Logic, and Tantrism. 

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Alongside Nalanda, Vikramshila was a premier institution of higher learning in ancient India. It played a pivotal role in the spread of Buddhist teachings, particularly Tantrism and Vajrayana Buddhism, across Asia, including Tibet. The Bhagalpur region itself is deeply historical, being one of the oldest districts, historically renowned as the 'Silk City' for its high-quality tussar silk fabric.

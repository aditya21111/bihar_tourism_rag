OVERVIEW:
Bihar Museum is located in Patna, planned as a history museum for the state of Bihar. Spread over 5.6 hectares with 24,000 square meters of built area, the museum exhibits historical artifacts from ancient India to 1764. Its highlights include the 2300-year-old Didarganj Yakshi, one of the finest sandstone statues in the world. It features a children's museum, an orientation theatre, and nine permanent galleries.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
The Bihar Museum is a major historical institution spread over 5.6 hectares with 24,000 square meters of built area. The museum includes a children's museum, a main entrance area, and an orientation theatre that were initially opened to the public, followed by the remaining galleries. It features nine permanent exhibition galleries showcasing artifacts from ancient India to 1764. Over 100 artifacts were transferred here from the older Patna Museum. The most significant exhibit is the 2300-year-old Didarganj Yakshi, a magnificent sandstone statue with a fly-whisk (chauri) held in the right hand (the left hand is broken), considered one of the finest in the world. Artifacts from the post-1764 period remain on display at the Patna Museum.

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The museum holds deep historical and cultural significance as it preserves artifacts spanning from ancient India to 1764, representing the rich heritage of the region. The inclusion of the 2300-year-old Didarganj Yakshi highlights the ancient sculptural brilliance of the Mauryan era.

OVERVIEW:
Guptadham is a highly revered natural cave situated in a valley of the Kaimur hills in Rohtas district. It is famous for a small rock formed by lime deposits that resembles a Shivling, known locally as Gupteshwar Mahadev. The cave is a major pilgrimage destination, where devotees pour holy water brought from the Ganges in Buxar onto the Shivling. Deep inside the cave lies a massive pit of stagnant water, known as Patalganga, making it a place of both spiritual and natural intrigue.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Located deeply within a valley of the Kaimur hill, Guptadham is a spectacular natural cave of significant religious importance. Inside the cave, visitors can find a unique small rock formed entirely of lime deposits that takes the natural shape of a Shivling. This formation is revered as Gupteshwar Mahadev. The cave complex holds a sense of mystery and devotion. About 363 feet deep inside the cave, there is a very large pit where stagnant water remains throughout the year. Due to the belief in its sacredness and mysterious underground location, this water pool is known as 'Patālgangā' (Subterranean River Ganges).

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Guptadham holds immense cultural and spiritual significance among devotees, primarily dedicated to Lord Shiva as Gupteshwar Mahadev. It serves as a prominent pilgrimage spot in the region.

LEGENDS, STORIES, OR TRADITIONS:
There is a strong religious tradition associated with Guptadham. Devotees carry holy water from the river Ganges in Buxar and travel to the cave to pour it on the natural Shivling as an offering.

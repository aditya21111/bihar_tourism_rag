OVERVIEW:
The Ancient Nalanda University Ruins showcase the remnants of the world's earliest residential international university. Established in the 5th century CE, it was the erstwhile center of global education and Buddhist learning. Today, it is Bihar's second World Heritage site and a sought-after destination for archaeologists, historians, and tourists.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION:
Built in red brick, the expansive ruins exhibit ancient stupas, viharas, and educational complexes. Originally covering a very large area, the university accommodated about 2000 teachers and 10,000 students of Buddhism. It reached the zenith of perfection in education and architecture during the periods of the Guptas and Palas. Detailed accounts of its grandeur were mentioned in the writings of the famous 7th century CE Chinese traveller Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang).

HISTORICAL OR CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Nalanda was the core of famous religions like Buddhism, Jainism, and Hinduism. The Gupta kings, Pala kings, and Harshavardhana were great patrons of this ancient city. It shone as a revered seat of Buddhism and an inspiration for quality in education worldwide.
